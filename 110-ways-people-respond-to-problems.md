# Ways People Respond to Problems
#meta, #learning, #leadership

![Photo by dev.to automation of a developer sitting in bliss with headphones on](images/110-01.jpeg)

On the blog, _Improve Something Today_, Brian Kerr wrote a fascinating post titled _[Three ways people respond to a problem (other than solving it)](https://improvesomething.today/responses-to-problems/)_. Drawing on his experience as a consultant, he surmised ways problems are addressed: **Push**, **Preserve**, or **Promote** them.

I appreciated the author's definitions and noticed that each section was titled with zero-padded numbering. Surely there are at least a thousand behaviors? So, as an invitation to extend the author's document, here is one more.

## No. 0004. Pretermit new problems

From my experience with software, when a new problem arises on a team, the most common reaction is to ignore it. "It's not my problem." However, there is always a small group within the team who will go for it; those devs illustrate Price's law — *the square root of the total number of contributors produces 50% of the work in any given domain.*

Deeper still, ignoring problems, especially when they are not an immediate threat, is a default human response. And in software, consulting, or business, it's about managing priorities and protecting egos.
